The stolen wing keeps what was taken: cut from its stretcher with a box cutter, lifted off a wall on a Monday morning, carried out under a coat while the fireworks went off. Some came back after decades, from bank vaults, from under beds, from a crime boss's house near Naples. Some never did. Here, the frames wait for both.

Warning: to avoid danger of forgetting, keep this page away from museum directors and insurance companies.

Notice: no frames were harmed in the making of this wing. most of them were already empty.

A Lady and Gentleman in Black

Rembrandt, 1633

At 1:24 a.m. two men dressed as Boston police rang the side door of the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum. The guard let them in. They tied up both guards in the basement.

They left at 2:45 with thirteen works. The reward is ten million dollars. The empty frames still hang where the paintings were, because Isabella's will says nothing may be moved.

On a Monday, when the Louvre was closed, a glazier who had helped put her behind glass walked out with her under his smock. Nobody noticed for a day. The guards thought she was being photographed.

Paris queued to look at the four empty hooks. Apollinaire was arrested, Picasso was questioned. She spent two years and four months in a trunk in Peruggia's room, until he tried to sell her to a dealer in Florence.
