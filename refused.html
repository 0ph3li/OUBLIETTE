Welcome to the refused

Home to Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe in spirit, if not in fact, the refused wing keeps everything a jury looked at for less than a second and sent back down the stairs.

The Salon hardly needs an introduction: it was the most-visited exhibition in the world, and it said no to more than three thousand pictures in 1863 alone.

Salon

1863

About five thousand pictures were sent to the Palais de l'Industrie that spring. The jury refused more than three thousand. The painters complained so loudly that Napoleon III went to look for himself, and decided the public could judge.

On 15 May 1863 the refused works opened in the rooms next door. Paris came to laugh, and kept coming back.

Manet

18321883

About

He wanted the Salon more than anyone. He sent them pictures for twenty years, and the jury kept sending some of them back.

In 1863 he hung Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe in the refused rooms, where it became the most mocked picture in Paris. In 1865 the jury let Olympia in, then had to guard her. In 1866 they refused The Fifer. In 1867 he gave up on them and built his own pavilion by the Pont de l'Alma, the way Courbet had twelve years before.

He never joined the Impressionists' shows. He kept knocking on the front door instead. They gave him the Légion d'honneur in 1881. He died two years later.

“He sent them pictures. They sent them back. He sent more.”— the keepers

Victorine

Meurent

1876

You know her face: she is Olympia, she is the woman on the grass. What nobody told you is that she painted too. In 1876 the Salon accepted her self-portrait, and refused both of Manet's pictures. Her self-portrait is lost. One painting of hers survives, in a small museum in Colombes.

Rome · Saint Peter's · April 1606

Palafrenieri

a few days on the altar

The papal grooms ordered an altarpiece for their chapel in the new basilica. Caravaggio gave them a Madonna with a low neckline, a naked Christ child old enough to walk, and Saint Anne as a wrinkled old woman watching them crush a snake.

It was hung, and then it was taken down. The grooms sold it, cheaply, to Cardinal Scipione Borghese, who had probably wanted it all along. It is still in his villa.
